FBR-2026-01 · independent examination memorandum

Fairfield City Schools
Bus disposals & maintenance records

The records establish sales and substantial maintenance history. They do not yet establish a complete, independently testable explanation for several material repair entries or the economic basis of the disposal decisions.

The major accountability gaps

Can taxpayers follow each bus from accurate identification, through condition and value assessment, to approval, transfer and payment? The supplied records leave material breaks in that trail.

Duplicate identifiers in the valuation sheet

Buses 5 and 11 repeat B658992. Buses 144 and 146 repeat B776032. The District acknowledged both errors. Other asset records identify Bus 11 with VIN ending 8993 and Bus 146 with ending 6034. This is a documented accuracy problem in a spreadsheet supplied as support for disposal decisions; not evidence that the physical buses carried duplicate VINs. Finding 06 and sources.

$1,000 per bus establishes the price; not the justification

The invoice, correspondence and receipt document 11 LKQ buses for $11,000. The reviewed production does not establish a complete vehicle-specific comparison supporting that disposal route and net return. It does not establish whether Doug Palmer physically inspected each bus, used photographs or relied on another assessment. Bus 58 is labeled “good for age” and Bus 68 “average for age,” yet both are designated “SCRAP.” Those labels require an explanation of the condition criteria and disposal decision; they do not prove roadworthiness.

What alternatives were evaluated, who accepted the particular price under what authority, and what assessment supported it? Board disposal approvals exist. The missing connection is the documented economic rationale and price-specific approval trail. “Replaced by a newer bus” does not supply that analysis. Finding 03 · Finding 04 · Finding 11.

The requests identified asset tags, VINs, invoice numbers, work orders and amounts. The District’s final response generally states “no additional responsive records were located.” Closing the search did not resolve these substantive questions. That wording does not establish deliberate withholding or that the records never existed. Request history and status matrix · District’s final response.

Additional reconciliation problems

Seven assets were labeled “Disposed Of” although the District said they remained in use. Their retained status was explained; the asset-record status still needs reconciliation. Separately, the newly supplied Bus 123 report differs from its dated detail by $271.33 and two lines, and includes a $7,927.71 generic entry. These Bus 123 exceptions were newly identified in this review and have not yet received a District response. Finding 05 · Finding 07.

Central assessment: The District produced substantial records, but the production still does not provide a complete, verifiable trail showing how certain repair charges were supported, how disposal values were justified, and how the chosen transactions protected taxpayers’ interests. Repeated, specifically identified requests ended with unresolved documentation gaps; not a complete reconciliation. No quantified loss, intentional concealment or favoritism is established by this review.

Transparency must allow verification

Releasing a large volume of records is not the same as providing a complete, traceable explanation of public spending. The practical test is whether a reader can connect an asset and recorded charge to its supporting invoice, authorization, work performed, adjustments and payment; and connect a disposal decision to condition, value, approval and proceeds.

For the specifically disputed Bus 33 and Bus 68 entries, that chain remains incomplete. Work-order numbers and summary charges were supplied, but the requested underlying itemized repair invoices and supporting financial records have not been located in the production. The final response reports no additional records located and closes the request. The search is closed; those financial questions are not resolved.

The District did supply useful records and resolve some questions. Nevertheless, the missing transaction support, acknowledged identifier errors and incomplete valuation rationale limit independent scrutiny. This is a concrete transparency shortcoming in the produced record, not evidence by itself of intentional concealment. No specific District slogan or public transparency pledge is quoted here without its source.

Examine every extracted repair entry and the flagged discrepancies · Compare recorded repair totals by bus and year · Read the request and response trail.

Nature of this work. Independent public-records analysis, prepared for public scrutiny. Not an IRS report, CPA audit, government investigation, attestation, appraisal or legal opinion. No audit assurance is expressed. Review cutoff: October 14, 2026.

Executive assessment

The strongest supported criticism is an incomplete public audit trail; not a proven total of stolen or wasted money. A $23,336 generic parts entry remains unsupported by itemization in the production; two identical $836.62 sets of parts on one date remain unreconciled; the source valuation sheet contains acknowledged identifier errors; and the newly produced Bus 123 report has a detail-to-total difference. The accepted LKQ price is documented, but a complete vehicle-specific economic rationale for the selected disposal route has not been established.

The District did produce meaningful evidence. Its invoice identifies the LKQ buses and price; receipts document three payment batches; separate records clarify state/local bus numbers; and it explained that seven authorized-for-disposal buses remained in use. Those answers narrow the findings and must remain visible alongside the criticism.

Assessment: The production supports targeted concerns about transaction support, data reliability, asset-status reconciliation and the transparency of value decisions. It does not support a defensible dollar total for loss, a fraud accusation, a conclusion of favoritism, or a statement that all disposal approvals were absent.

Review scope and method

This review examines the locally assembled bus-disposal collection, the supplied email transcripts, the new Bus 123 workbook and the additional bus item listing. It extracts PDF text and embedded form fields, renders pages with form appearances, checks maintenance line totals, reconciles bus identities and separates original-request assets from other payment-batch vehicles. Source-file hashes are recorded. Photographs are reproduced from the supplied Word documents; they are not newly taken inspection photographs.

The review does not include direct access to the District’s accounting system, bank confirmation, vendor confirmation, physical inspections, interviews, complete procurement files, certified valuation, or a test of all relevant law and policies. The collection is the locally located production, not a certified inventory of every email or attachment ever sent. No claim that unlocated records never existed is made.

Finding 01 · documentary exception / accountability question

A $23,336 repair-history entry does not identify what was purchased

Documented condition
Bus 33 records $23,336 on October 28, 2021 under work order 00004-0027798, type OP, description “MISC PARTS OUT,” quantity 1. The supplied history reports a total of $37,803.37 for this bus; this one entry accounts for approximately 61.7% of that recorded total.
Review benchmark
A reviewer should be able to connect a material maintenance charge to an itemized description, authorization, receipt of goods or completed work, and the corresponding financial transaction. This is the review’s accountability benchmark, not an opinion on compliance with a particular audit standard.
District response / evidence supplied
The September 24 follow-up specifically identified the bus, work order, date and amount and requested invoices, itemization, authorizations, completion records and payment/credit records. Final Section 8 says no additional responsive invoices, payment records, credits or supporting financial records were located.
What remains unresolved
The supplied line establishes a recorded charge, but does not explain the goods or services, vendor invoice, approval, completion, or payment. The reason supporting records could not be located is not established.
Why this matters
Taxpayers cannot independently test whether this material charge was appropriate, accurately recorded, completed, and paid once.
What would resolve it
Identify the source document and accounting entry, itemize the work/parts, and reconcile any adjustments or credits. If records no longer exist, identify the documented retention/disposition history, if available.
Limit on the conclusion
This is not a proven $23,336 loss. A maintenance-system entry is not independently verified cash payment. The 2021 charge and 2025 sale are separated by years; the sale price does not retroactively make the repair wasteful.

Finding 02 · documentary exception / accountability question

Two work orders record the same $836.62 in parts on the same day

Documented condition
Bus 68 has work orders 00004-0020233 and 00004-0020237 dated November 8, 2018. Each lists a $12.11 relay (3607384C1) and an $824.51 outward-opening-door tube assembly (2201059C93). Each pair totals $836.62; both together total $1,673.24.
Review benchmark
Repeated charges should be traceable to distinct parts issues or services, or to a documented correction/reversal. Matching entries are an exception to reconcile, not a finding of duplicate payment by themselves.
District response / evidence supplied
The September 24 Section 8 request quoted both work-order numbers and both parts amounts, asking for work orders, parts-issue/return, invoice, credit and payment support. Final Section 8 reports no additional supporting financial records located.
What remains unresolved
The records supplied do not resolve whether these were two legitimate issues, a reposting, a corrected work order, or a duplicate charge.
Why this matters
An unresolved duplicate-appearing entry prevents a reliable determination of the actual net repair cost.
What would resolve it
Trace both work orders to quantities issued/returned, invoices and cash disbursements; show any reversal or credit.
Limit on the conclusion
Do not call $1,673.24 an overpayment. If one of the two sets were ultimately proven duplicated and paid without reversal, the duplicated set would be $836.62. Neither proposition has been established. Bus 68 was outside the original 27-asset scope but was included in the supplied LKQ records.

Finding 03 · documentary exception / accountability question

The $1,000-per-bus price is documented; its economic justification remains incomplete

Documented condition
Invoice 2125, dated April 14, 2025, identifies 11 buses sold to LKQ for $11,000. A March 19, 2025 email from LKQ representative William Messer to Lance Perry, copying Doug Palmer, says he spoke with Doug and asks the District to invoice $1,000 each. Receipt 251040 records $11,000 on May 15, 2025.
Review benchmark
An assessable disposal decision connects condition and disposition options to the accepted offer, authorized decision-maker, and proceeds. A buyer’s agreed price establishes terms; it does not establish market value or the best available alternative.
District response / evidence supplied
The District produced the invoice and correspondence on October 5. It described FAIRFIELD TRADES as an existing spreadsheet responsive in part to condition, estimated mileage, and disposal method/value considered. Final Section 9 says no additional responsive bids, offers, quotations, auction records, pickup records, approvals or related disposal records were located beyond those already provided.
What remains unresolved
A complete contemporaneous comparison of resale, auction, trade and recycling returns, including costs and vehicle-specific constraints, has not been established in the reviewed production. The supplied materials do not establish whether Doug Palmer inspected each bus physically, used photographs, or relied on another assessment.
Why this matters
The public can verify the agreed price but cannot independently conclude that it was the best reasonable net return.
What would resolve it
Identify the evaluation supporting the chosen disposal route and net price, who accepted it under what authority, and the dated inspection/assessment relied upon. An existing documented reason for rejecting alternatives would resolve the issue more directly than a general assurance.
Limit on the conclusion
No independent contemporaneous appraisal was performed for this review. Calling the sale “pennies on the dollar” would require a defensible value denominator. No quantified underpricing loss is established.

Finding 04 · documentary exception / accountability question

“Replaced by a newer bus” is a reason for replacement, not a cost-benefit analysis

Documented condition
The 27 asset-numbered disposal forms contain Business Operations as the disposing department, “Replaced by a newer bus” as the reason, and the “Sold” checkbox. Their embedded representative-signature metadata names Lance Perry and carries August 13, 2024; the displayed date field is 8-13-2024. The Treasurer/Designee signature field has no populated value in these copies. Board disposal approvals are separately documented under resolutions 24-84 and 24-104.
Review benchmark
Replacement should be supported by serviceability, safety, reliability and lifecycle economics. Authorization to dispose of an asset should be distinguished from authorization to accept a particular buyer and price.
District response / evidence supplied
The District supplied disposal forms, fixed-asset records, older fleet analysis, maintenance histories and later transaction records. The final response reports no additional inspection/condition or disposal-method/price records beyond those previously provided.
What remains unresolved
The generic replacement reason does not itself establish why a given bus should be scrapped rather than retained or offered for resale, or the economic comparison with keeping it. A blank signature field on these copies does not establish that approval was absent elsewhere. The connection between Board authorization, administrative processing and specific price acceptance remains insufficiently explained for independent review.
Why this matters
A disposal process can be formally authorized yet still lack a publicly traceable explanation of value-for-money.
What would resolve it
Identify the contemporaneous replacement recommendation and economic/condition basis, the applicable authorization/delegation and price acceptance, and where the Treasurer approval required by the form is documented.
Limit on the conclusion
The signature metadata was read, not cryptographically authenticated. Identical template dates or unfilled fields do not establish forgery or unauthorized disposal. The recorded Board approvals must not be omitted from any allegation.

Sources: 200015.pdf · form and embedded fields; representative example; 210193.pdf · form and embedded fields; Bus Disposition Records.pdf · authorized_by column; Board approval excerpts · 24-84 and 24-104; Final response · Sections 5 and 9

Finding 05 · documentary exception / accountability question

“Disposed Of” in the asset listing conflicts with seven buses reportedly still in use

Documented condition
The supplied bus item listing labels all 27 requested assets “Disposed Of.” On September 28, the District stated that buses 30, 123, 127, 128, 130, 137 and 146 had not actually been disposed of, remained on District property and were still in use. Bus 123’s newly supplied history includes entries into July 2026.
Review benchmark
Asset status should distinguish authorization for disposal, accounting retirement and physical transfer, with a cross-reference where those dates or statuses differ.
District response / evidence supplied
The District explicitly explained that authorization did not mean actual completed disposition and that no completed-sale proceeds existed for those seven buses. Final Section 4 reiterates that prior explanation.
What remains unresolved
The explanation resolves the supposed “missing buses,” but leaves the public-facing asset status and actual use inconsistent without a documented status bridge. The correct accounting treatment cannot be determined from these records alone.
Why this matters
Readers can mistake administrative disposal entries for completed transfers, distort proceeds comparisons, or lose sight of assets still controlled and used.
What would resolve it
Reconcile asset-system status and actual custody/use for the seven buses, including the effective dates and the applicable accounting treatment.
Limit on the conclusion
This is not evidence that seven buses disappeared. It is not a conclusion that the financial statements are misstated; the posting basis and full accounting records were not tested.

Sources: Records request - bus item listing.csv · status column; Email chain supplied - 13.txt · September 28 response; Repair History - FAIRFIELD CITY BUS 123 (1) (1).xlsx · 2025–2026 entries; Final response · Section 4

Finding 06 · documentary exception / accountability question

Valuation-sheet identifiers and condition labels need qualification

Documented condition
FAIRFIELD TRADES repeats B658992 for buses 5 and 11 and B776032 for buses 144 and 146. The District expressly acknowledged two VIN repeats. The bus item listing identifies Bus 11 as VIN ending 8993 and Bus 146 as ending 6034, rather than the repeated suffixes. The same valuation sheet calls Bus 58 “GFA” (good for age) and Bus 68 “AFA” (average for age), while listing both as “SCRAP.”
Review benchmark
A valuation or condition schedule should identify the correct asset and make clear what the condition classification means for the selected disposal route.
District response / evidence supplied
The District supplied the spreadsheet as maintained, acknowledged the duplicate identifiers and did not alter it for production. Separate records and its September 28 email resolve local/state numbering for buses 5, 11 and 25. Historical condition and maintenance information was supplied.
What remains unresolved
The duplicate identifiers reduce confidence in using this sheet alone. “Good for age” or “average for age” and “SCRAP” are not necessarily contradictory, but the sheet does not explain the decision threshold or mechanical limitations behind those classifications.
Why this matters
Using the schedule without cross-checking can attribute value or condition to the wrong bus or overstate what the condition label proves.
What would resolve it
Use full VINs from the asset listing and transaction documents; identify dated condition criteria and the basis for SCRAP classifications. Explain the assessment method and assessor’s role.
Limit on the conclusion
The repeated values are not proof that the physical vehicles had duplicate VINs. The photographs and condition labels do not establish roadworthiness, market value or fitness for continued student transportation.

Sources: FAIRFIELD TRADES.xlsx · rows for 5/11, 144/146, 58 and 68; Records request - bus item listing.csv · full VINs; Email chain supplied - 13.txt · October 5 acknowledgment and September 28 clarification

Finding 07 · documentary exception / accountability question

Bus 123’s newly supplied report does not reconcile detail to its total

Documented condition
The Bus 123 workbook contains 118 dated detail rows totaling $29,342.09. Its asset/report total is $29,613.42 and its reported line count is 120. The difference is $271.33 and two lines. The detail description is repeatedly “FF CITY - TRANS,” including a $7,927.71 entry on June 9, 2025, work order 0031654 (1). The report header includes “Filter value index is out of range.”
Review benchmark
A repair-history report should reconcile its displayed detail to totals and allow a material entry to be understood or traced to supporting detail.
District response / evidence supplied
The final response identifies this attachment as the remaining maintenance history for Bus 123. The workbook was generated October 13, 2026; the final email’s sent-date header was not supplied in this conversation.
What remains unresolved
The visible dated detail and reported total differ. A generic transportation description does not disclose the particular parts or work. The filter warning raises a report-completeness question, but does not by itself explain the mismatch.
Why this matters
The reader cannot reproduce the reported total from the displayed dated entries or evaluate the large 2025 entry from this summary alone.
What would resolve it
Reconcile the two additional lines/$271.33 and identify the underlying itemized work order for $7,927.71. Explain the filter warning and whether the export omitted details.
Limit on the conclusion
These are newly identified exceptions in this review. They were not the Bus 33/68 issues put to the District in September. No subsequent District response to these Bus 123 questions is available. Bus 123 was reportedly retained, so its 2025 repair is not evidence of repairing a bus immediately before scrapping it.

Finding 08 · documentary exception / accountability question

The request trail shows specific, repeated efforts; not just a general demand for answers

Documented condition
The September 13 request identified 27 asset tags and sought final disposition/proceeds records. September 22 requested specific links, Invoice 2125 and inspection/condition documents. September 24 separated nine sections, named work orders and VINs, and asked the District to distinguish records supplied, not located or still under search. The September 23 response had reported no additional records in categories including Invoice 2125; the invoice and correspondence were later produced October 5.
Review benchmark
A transparent records trail should allow a reader to distinguish an initial search result from a later production and a final disposition of each request category.
District response / evidence supplied
The District supplied records in installments, clarified vehicle identities and retained status, produced Invoice 2125 later, acknowledged valuation-sheet errors, and ultimately stated its search was complete. The final response says no additional records were located for several remaining categories.
What remains unresolved
Closing the records search did not resolve the substantive Bus 33/68 support questions or establish a complete value/condition rationale. Earlier no-additional-records language did not mean the invoice could not later be found.
Why this matters
A large volume of records can create the appearance of completeness while specific material questions remain unresolved. Conversely, calling the production wholly unresponsive would ignore real records and answers.
What would resolve it
Publish a precise exception register and identify the supplied record that resolves each remaining issue, or accurately record that the support was not located. Distinguish “not located,” “not maintained,” “destroyed,” and “withheld”; they are not interchangeable.
Limit on the conclusion
The chronology does not prove intentional concealment, bad faith, destruction of records, or a public-records-law violation. No legal compliance opinion is issued.

Sources: Email chain supplied - 13.txt · September 22–October 5 chain; Final response · all categories

Finding 09 · documentary exception / accountability question

Mileage fields cannot be treated as a consistent dated vehicle history

Documented condition
The September 24 follow-up identified March 2022 fleet-sheet mileage higher than the odometer field printed in March 2025 maintenance reports: Bus 31, 161,297 versus 157,588; Bus 43, 157,438 versus 138,038; Bus 51, 126,504 versus 82,363; Bus 67, 164,461 versus 153,313. It also identified the Bus 43/42 partial-VIN conflict in the older fleet workbook.
Review benchmark
Mileage and identity fields used for disposal should be tied to a date, full VIN and source, rather than assuming the report print date equals the observation date.
District response / evidence supplied
The District provided additional Bus 43 vehicle records and later said no additional responsive mileage-correction, odometer/cluster-replacement or identification-correction records were located beyond those supplied.
What remains unresolved
The request trail documents the differences; their explanation and final pre-transfer mileage remain insufficiently linked in the supplied summaries. The older partial-VIN conflict is addressed by other full-VIN records and should not be treated as proof of a missing vehicle.
Why this matters
Unqualified mileage figures can mislead valuation and repair-per-mile comparisons.
What would resolve it
Identify the dated observation underlying each mileage field and the last pre-transfer odometer record, with any correction or replacement record if one exists.
Limit on the conclusion
A later print date does not mean a later odometer reading. These differences do not establish odometer rollback or tampering.

Finding 10 · documentary exception / accountability question

Historical maintenance totals are useful context, not a calculated loss

Documented condition
The 11 LKQ buses have $247,424.02 in recorded maintenance-history line amounts over the available period July 2014–February 2025. The histories include parts/outside-parts amounts; labor lines generally carry zero dollars. The 11-bus sale was $11,000. These are different measures across different time periods.
Review benchmark
A repair-versus-replace assessment requires timing, condition, remaining service life, downtime, avoided future costs and the cost of alternatives. Lifetime maintenance should not be subtracted from disposal proceeds as a loss calculation.
District response / evidence supplied
The District supplied the 11 histories and later the additional histories. The “Please do not ask for maintenance files” note in FAIRFIELD TRADES did not prevent production of the 11 histories in the reviewed collection.
What remains unresolved
The public production provides substantial transaction-level history but does not establish a complete lifecycle economic comparison. A zero labor-dollar column is not evidence that labor was free.
Why this matters
A dramatic but invalid maintenance-minus-sale comparison would overstate waste and obscure the genuine unsupported-entry and decision-support questions.
What would resolve it
Use the bus/year schedule and source work orders to isolate material repairs near a documented disposal decision; obtain the prospective economic assessment and fully loaded costs before drawing a loss conclusion.
Limit on the conclusion
No total for fraud, waste, improper payments, avoidable spending or forgone sale proceeds is established. Public accountability concerns remain even when a dollar loss cannot yet be measured.

Sources: FAIRFIELD TRADES.xlsx · maintenance note; BUS 33.pdf · history; Billing (6) (1).pdf · sale total

Finding 11 · documentary exception / accountability question

The photographic files are not a complete inspection record

Documented condition
The 11 supplied bus Word files contain 86 embedded images across seven files: buses 31, 33, 42, 43, 57, 58 and 67. Files named for buses 48, 51, 68 and 72 contain no embedded raster images. The file named Bus 51.docx contains only the text “Bus 57” and no identification table. Word document properties name Doug Palmer as author/last modifier, with March 18, 2025 creation/modification dates.
Review benchmark
A vehicle assessment should clearly identify the bus, inspection date, assessor, scope, findings and decision consequence. Photographs and document-author fields are not substitutes for a documented mechanical/structural assessment.
District response / evidence supplied
The District supplied these files and additional historical vehicle and maintenance records. Its final response says no additional inspection/reinspection/condition records were located beyond those previously supplied.
What remains unresolved
The files illustrate visible exterior, interior, engine-compartment and identification details for some buses; they do not provide the same photographic coverage for all 11. The filename/content mismatch for Bus 51 requires care. No evidence in these Word files establishes whether Palmer personally inspected every bus or whether the photos were the sole input to pricing.
Why this matters
Treating all 11 Word documents as 11 photographed/inspected buses would overstate the evidence. Treating an apparently intact exterior as proof of roadworthiness would also overstate it.
What would resolve it
Identify the assessment scope and dated findings used for pricing, correct the Bus 51 file identification, and distinguish photographed vehicles from physically inspected vehicles.
Limit on the conclusion
Metadata is a lead about document preparation, not proof of who took photographs or performed inspections. No conclusion that photos were the only assessment evidence is made.

Sources: Bus 51.docx · filename versus body; Bus 31.docx · photos and metadata; Bus 48.docx · text-only record; Bus 68.docx · text-only record; Bus 72.docx · text-only record; Final response · Section 5

Decision and accountability questions

  1. What specifically was purchased under Bus 33’s $23,336 entry, and which documents establish authorization, completion and payment?
  2. What differentiates the two Bus 68 parts sets, or where is their reversal/credit?
  3. Who evaluated the $1,000-per-bus offer, under what delegated authority, and against what alternatives?
  4. What dated physical or documentary assessment supports the SCRAP designations, especially for buses labeled good/average for age?
  5. How did “Replaced by a newer bus” translate into an economic decision that protected taxpayers?
  6. Where is the required Treasurer/Designee approval documented, and how does it relate to the Board approvals and specific sale terms?
  7. Why did seven in-use buses remain labeled “Disposed Of” in the supplied listing?
  8. What accounts for the missing detail behind Bus 123’s $271.33 difference and $7,927.71 summary entry?
  9. What records-retention and search facts explain why the requested support could not be located?

These are substantive accountability questions, not a claim that public-records law obligates the District to create new explanations or analyses. A targeted response or an existing source record could resolve or change a finding.

Finding 08 · documentary exception / accountability question

The request trail shows specific, repeated efforts; not just a general demand for answers

Documented condition
The September 13 request identified 27 asset tags and sought final disposition/proceeds records. September 22 requested specific links, Invoice 2125 and inspection/condition documents. September 24 separated nine sections, named work orders and VINs, and asked the District to distinguish records supplied, not located or still under search. The September 23 response had reported no additional records in categories including Invoice 2125; the invoice and correspondence were later produced October 5.
Review benchmark
A transparent records trail should allow a reader to distinguish an initial search result from a later production and a final disposition of each request category.
District response / evidence supplied
The District supplied records in installments, clarified vehicle identities and retained status, produced Invoice 2125 later, acknowledged valuation-sheet errors, and ultimately stated its search was complete. The final response says no additional records were located for several remaining categories.
What remains unresolved
Closing the records search did not resolve the substantive Bus 33/68 support questions or establish a complete value/condition rationale. Earlier no-additional-records language did not mean the invoice could not later be found.
Why this matters
A large volume of records can create the appearance of completeness while specific material questions remain unresolved. Conversely, calling the production wholly unresponsive would ignore real records and answers.
What would resolve it
Publish a precise exception register and identify the supplied record that resolves each remaining issue, or accurately record that the support was not located. Distinguish “not located,” “not maintained,” “destroyed,” and “withheld”; they are not interchangeable.
Limit on the conclusion
The chronology does not prove intentional concealment, bad faith, destruction of records, or a public-records-law violation. No legal compliance opinion is issued.

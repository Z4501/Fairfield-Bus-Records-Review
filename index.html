The major accountability gaps

Can taxpayers follow each bus from accurate identification, through condition and value assessment, to approval, transfer and payment? The supplied records leave material breaks in that trail.

Duplicate identifiers in the valuation sheet

Buses 5 and 11 repeat B658992. Buses 144 and 146 repeat B776032. The District acknowledged both errors. Other asset records identify Bus 11 with VIN ending 8993 and Bus 146 with ending 6034. This is a documented accuracy problem in a spreadsheet supplied as support for disposal decisions—not evidence that the physical buses carried duplicate VINs. Finding 06 and sources.

$1,000 per bus establishes the price—not the justification

The invoice, correspondence and receipt document 11 LKQ buses for $11,000. The reviewed production does not establish a complete vehicle-specific comparison supporting that disposal route and net return. It does not establish whether Doug Palmer physically inspected each bus, used photographs or relied on another assessment. Bus 58 is labeled “good for age” and Bus 68 “average for age,” yet both are designated “SCRAP.” Those labels require an explanation of the condition criteria and disposal decision; they do not prove roadworthiness.

What alternatives were evaluated, who accepted the particular price under what authority, and what assessment supported it? Board disposal approvals exist. The missing connection is the documented economic rationale and price-specific approval trail. “Replaced by a newer bus” does not supply that analysis. Finding 03 · Finding 04 · Finding 11.

Additional reconciliation problems

Seven assets were labeled “Disposed Of” although the District said they remained in use. Their retained status was explained; the asset-record status still needs reconciliation. Separately, the newly supplied Bus 123 report differs from its dated detail by $271.33 and two lines, and includes a $7,927.71 generic entry. These Bus 123 exceptions were newly identified in this review and have not yet received a District response. Finding 05 · Finding 07.

Central assessment: The District produced substantial records, but the production still does not provide a complete, verifiable trail showing how certain repair charges were supported, how disposal values were justified, and how the chosen transactions protected taxpayers’ interests. Repeated, specifically identified requests ended with unresolved documentation gaps—not a complete reconciliation. No quantified loss, intentional concealment or favoritism is established by this review.

Transparency must allow verification

Releasing a large volume of records is not the same as providing a complete, traceable explanation of public spending. The practical test is whether a reader can connect an asset and recorded charge to its supporting invoice, authorization, work performed, adjustments and payment—and connect a disposal decision to condition, value, approval and proceeds.

For the specifically disputed Bus 33 and Bus 68 entries, that chain remains incomplete. Work-order numbers and summary charges were supplied, but the requested underlying itemized repair invoices and supporting financial records have not been located in the production. The final response reports no additional records located and closes the request. The search is closed; those financial questions are not resolved.

The District did supply useful records and resolve some questions. Nevertheless, the missing transaction support, acknowledged identifier errors and incomplete valuation rationale limit independent scrutiny. This is a concrete transparency shortcoming in the produced record, not evidence by itself of intentional concealment. No specific District slogan or public transparency pledge is quoted here without its source.
